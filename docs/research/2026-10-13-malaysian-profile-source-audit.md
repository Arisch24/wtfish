# Malaysian freshwater and marine source audit

Reviewed 13 October 2026. Profile sentences were written anew from factual findings; source prose and illustrations were not reproduced. This note records source choices and uncertainty, not new fisheries rules.

## Source reuse

MyBIS's species-page footer includes “information or images” and says they “shall not be used without prior written approval from the owner(s).” See [the footer](https://www.mybis.gov.my/sp/23929). This broad restriction is a reason to prefer other provenance; it is not a finding that simple scientific facts or names are proprietary. The design expressly permits government facts in original wording.

MyBIS links were removed from the profiles audited here. Malay names and Malaysian occurrence are supported instead by DOF, LKIM, and primary research. No MyBIS images were downloaded. FishBase and SeriouslyFish prose was not used. IUCN categories remain imported from Wikidata rather than from the Red List API.

## Freshwater profiles

Twenty-five new files cover the three freshwater/estuary batches. Their summaries, habitat descriptions, diagnostic features and food-use text are original. Unsupported maximum sizes, numerical aquarium guidance, local fishing rules, baits and seasons remain absent.

The [DOF native freshwater poster](https://www.dof.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/SPESIS-TEMPATAN-POSTER-A1.pdf) confirms native Malaysian species and names including sebarau, toman, haruan, temoleh, kelisa, jelawat, baung, lampam sungai, tengas, puyu, belida, patin buah, kaloi and sepat ronggeng. The [2023 Kenyir primary checklist](https://doi.org/10.3897/BDJ.11.e100337), published under CC BY, independently supports many native species and distinguishes fish introduced *into that reservoir* from fish introduced *into Malaysia*.

Taxonomic and naming decisions:

- `malayan-river-catfish` replaces the planned `asian-redtail-catfish` native profile. It describes **Hemibagrus capitulum**, the Malaysian baung formerly frequently misidentified as **H. nemurus**. The [Catalog entry](https://researcharchive.calacademy.org/research/ichthyology/catalog/fishcatget.asp?spid=54621), [2021 Ulu Paip primary checklist](https://jms.mabjournal.com/index.php/mab/article/download/11/5/18), and DOF poster support the correction. Modern **H. nemurus** is restricted to Java.
- `wallago-catfish` uses the currently accepted **Wallagonia leerii**, with old spellings included as other names. See [Catalog](https://researcharchive.calacademy.org/research/ichthyology/catalog/fishcatget.asp?spid=5957).
- **Tor tambroides** remains accepted in the [Catalog](https://researcharchive.calacademy.org/research/ichthyology/catalog/fishcatget.asp?spid=31936), but Malaysian *Tor* identifications are disputed. The profile explains uncertainty rather than assuming every kelah is this species. The DOF poster labels kelah **Tor tambra**.
- `giant-featherback` retains **Chitala lopis**. Its profile distinguishes the species from uncertain Malaysian **Chitala cf. lopis** records. The [Catalog](https://researcharchive.calacademy.org/research/ichthyology/catalog/fishcatget.asp?spid=10125) and [2023 rediscovery paper](https://www.int-res.com/articles/esr2023/52/n052p285.pdf) document that the old extinct assessment does not mean there are no living fish. [Recent molecular work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13093427/) also discusses multiple *Chitala* species in Peninsular Malaysia.
- `pangasius-nasutus` keeps its scientific filename because “river catfish” is generic and ambiguous. [DOF's FAO-hosted cryopreservation document](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/abdc/documents/cryofish.pdf) uses that name and Patin Buah; a specific unique English name was not established. The profile differentiates it from introduced **Pangasianodon hypophthalmus**.
- `three-spot-gourami` uses the DOF headline name **Sepat ronggeng**. The planned “Sepat benang” could not be verified against a primary source and was omitted.
- `soro-brook-carp` does not claim that absence of a fleshy lip always distinguishes it from kelah. [Original taxonomic work](https://www.mapress.com/zootaxa/2015/f/zt03962p157.pdf) documents multiple mouth morphs, including a lobe form.

The **Hemibagrus wyckioides** profile treats it as invasive, following DOF and primary Malaysian research. It does not repeat the plan's asserted blanket ban effective 1 July 2024: the [DOF import-risk page](https://www.dof.gov.my/perkhidmatan/import-eksport/permohonan-ira-bagi-spesis-ikan-alien/) describes import with controls. DOF's 2026 seizure report explicitly names *Amazon* redtail catfish, which is another species. Without a verified species-level gazette, no equivalent blanket legal claim was added.

Estuary naming follows the [DOF 2009 marine names guide](https://repository.seafdec.org.my/bitstream/handle/20.500.12561/1010/Valid%20Local%20Name%20of%20Malaysia%20Marine%20Fishes_DOF_2.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1): John's snapper is **Jenahak tanda** with Jenahak as an alias; fourfinger threadfin is **Senangin rambut empat**, with Kurau janggut and Kurau pipih as aliases. The guide includes Siakap merah among names for mangrove jack, whose preferred guide name is Kakap merah.

## Marine source replacement audit

The following eight profiles had MyBIS source links replaced, preserving all other fields, generated data and photos:

| Profile | Replacement provenance |
| --- | --- |
| Talang queenfish | DOF 2009 names guide and DOF marine identification guide: *Scomberoides commersonnianus*, Talang lima jari/Talang/Seliat |
| Narrow-barred Spanish mackerel | Existing LKIM commercial fish book plus DOF 2009 names guide |
| Brown-marbled grouper | [Primary Malaysian specimen study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4519877/) and [UMS parasitology thesis](https://eprints.ums.edu.my/id/eprint/37633/1/24%20PAGES.pdf), which uses Kerapu harimau for *E. fuscoguttatus* |
| Giant grouper | Existing LKIM commercial fish book and DOF 2009 names guide |
| Indian mackerel | LKIM commercial fish book, [2023 commercial-species poster](https://www.lkim.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Poster_Ikan.2023-1.pdf), and [MyCC fisheries market review](https://www.mycc.gov.my/sites/default/files/pdf/newsroom/MyCC%20Report%20Market%20Review%20on%20Food%20Sector_eBook.pdf), explicitly documenting Malaysian waters and Kembung borek |
| Kawakawa | Existing DOF tuna licensing page plus LKIM 2023 commercial-species poster: Aya kurik / *Euthynnus affinis* |
| Humphead wrasse | [Fishes of Australia species account](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/2962) for biology and [UMS thesis](https://eprints.ums.edu.my/id/eprint/41255/1/24%20PAGES.pdf) explicitly describing Sabah as natural habitat and Mameng as a local name |
| Indo-Pacific sailfish | DOF 2009 guide: Layaran Pasifik/Layaran, plus Tourism Malaysia's sport-fishing guide and existing Malaysian charter evidence |

Giant trevally, hound needlefish, cobia, black pomfret, yellowstripe scad and barramundi already had non-MyBIS primary/local provenance and needed no replacement.

Marine `native` classifications combine the established natural Indo-Pacific range with Malaysian wild/local fisheries records. Where a source establishes local occurrence but does not literally label the fish native, this is an inference from those combined facts. Market presence alone was not treated as proof that a fish was introduced.

The scanned DOF 2009 PDF has no extractable text in the locally downloaded copy; indexed text has occasional shifted rows or older taxonomic names. Species-specific local-name findings were cross-checked against additional DOF, LKIM or primary Malaysian research where possible. In particular, a search snippet pairing *E. fuscoguttatus* with “Itajara” was not treated as authoritative; the UMS source confirms Kerapu harimau.
